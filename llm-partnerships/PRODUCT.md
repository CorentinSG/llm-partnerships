# Product

## Register

product

## Users

French law students, young lawyers, and advisors comparing LL.M pathways between French universities and foreign law schools. They arrive with practical questions: which partnerships exist, what they cost, which requirements apply, how reliable the information is, and which official source should be checked before applying.

## Product Purpose

LL.M Partnerships is a decision-support directory for comparing international LL.M partnerships. Success means a user can quickly filter, compare, open details, estimate cost, inspect reliability, and move toward official university sources without losing trust in the data.

## Brand Personality

Clear, expert, composed. The interface should feel modern and premium, but never decorative at the expense of comprehension. It should carry legal-academic credibility while feeling lighter and more contemporary than a university PDF.

## Anti-references

Avoid generic SaaS landing pages, decorative gradient blobs, glass everywhere, oversized hero cards, vague AI-tool aesthetics, novelty motion that slows comparison, and institutional pages that bury useful data in long prose. Do not change routes, data semantics, filter behavior, form fields, or analytics-relevant labels without explicit approval.

## Design Principles

1. Preserve the task: every visual decision must support search, filtering, comparison, or source verification.
2. Make uncertainty legible: reliability status, unofficial sources, unknown fields, and estimates must be visually clear without alarming users unnecessarily.
3. Use motion for orientation: transitions should show state changes, panel relationships, and feedback while staying responsive.
4. Keep density elegant: the product can be data-rich, but spacing, typography, and grouping should make it scannable.
5. Treat trust as the brand: legal disclaimers, source links, and cost estimates should feel integrated, not tacked on.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG AA for contrast, focus states, keyboard navigation, touch targets, and readable type. Respect `prefers-reduced-motion` by keeping fades/color transitions while removing movement-heavy effects. Avoid relying on color alone for reliability, warnings, or selected states.
