# Product

## Register

product

## Users

French law students, young lawyers, and advisors comparing LL.M pathways between French universities and foreign law schools. They need to filter, compare, understand costs, judge reliability, and reach official sources quickly.

## Product Purpose

LL.M Partnerships is a decision-support directory for comparing international LL.M partnerships. Success means a user can quickly search, filter, compare, open details, estimate cost, inspect reliability, and move toward official university sources without losing trust in the data.

## Brand Personality

Clear, expert, composed, premium, and future-facing. The interface should feel like a refined legal research command center, not a generic SaaS landing page or a university PDF.

## Anti-references

Avoid decorative gradient blobs, glass everywhere, oversized hero cards, vague AI-tool aesthetics, novelty motion that slows comparison, and institutional pages that bury useful data in long prose. Do not change routes, data semantics, filter behavior, form fields, or analytics-relevant labels without explicit approval.

## Design Principles

1. Preserve the task: every visual decision must support search, filtering, comparison, cost understanding, or source verification.
2. Make uncertainty legible: reliability status, unofficial sources, unknown fields, and estimates must be visually clear without alarming users unnecessarily.
3. Use motion for orientation: transitions should show state changes, panel relationships, and feedback while staying responsive.
4. Keep density elegant: the product can be data-rich, but spacing, typography, and grouping should make it scannable.
5. Treat trust as the brand: legal disclaimers, source links, and cost estimates should feel integrated, not tacked on.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG AA for contrast, focus states, keyboard navigation, touch targets, and readable type. Respect `prefers-reduced-motion` by keeping fades/color transitions while removing movement-heavy effects. Avoid relying on color alone for reliability, warnings, or selected states.
